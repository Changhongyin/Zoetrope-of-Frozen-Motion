Audit the actual available tools and hardware briefly. Do not reinstall functioning software or spend the project researching tools. Use documented, available capabilities; do not assume a particular model release grants tools you do not have.

## 4. Produce and polish one complete work

Make the entry understandable through the experience, not a wall of explanatory text. Use only the secondary details that strengthen the core idea.

Where assets are viewed closely, give them enough geometry, texture and material quality for those shots. Match camera distance to asset quality. Control clipping, intersections, lighting, transparency, transitions and distracting repetition where they matter to the final presentation. Do not hide a broken core behind an attractive poster or an unrelated prerecorded render.

Do not assume the VM still has an old CPU/RAM allocation, start competing heavy renders, or disable memory guards to force a job through. Reduce optional complexity before sacrificing the signature experience.

Fix concrete visible or functional defects. Do not enter endless whole-scene parameter sweeps, alternative versions or minor aesthetic revisions after acceptance criteria are met. A technically and aesthetically coherent completed piece takes priority over additional features.
